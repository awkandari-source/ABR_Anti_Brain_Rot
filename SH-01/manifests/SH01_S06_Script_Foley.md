## Sanitized Continuous Narration Script

The instant you stand in a quiet line or finish a task, reaching for a fast-scrolling screen interrupts one of your brain's most restorative circuits. Neuroimaging research reviewed by Harvard Health Publishing and the National Library of Medicine shows that when external stimulation drops, your brain does not power down; instead, it activates the Default Mode Network. Across linked prefrontal, parietal, and memory regions, fifteen unhurried minutes of quiet boredom allow your cortex to consolidate recent learning, weave autobiographical memories together, and connect distant ideas into original insights. Constant rapid-cut feeds keep your attention locked in reactive alert mode, whereas resting your gaze out a rainy window gives tired executive networks the calm margin they need to reset.
