## Sanitized Continuous Narration Script

While monarch butterflies migrate thousands of miles south each autumn, the banded woolly bear caterpillar simply curls beneath fallen oak bark and lets winter freeze it nearly solid. As temperatures drop, biologists with the United States Fish and Wildlife Service and the National Weather Service explain that this fuzzy Isabella tiger moth larva floods its tissues with glycerol, a natural sugar alcohol cryoprotectant. Ice crystals form safely in the fluid spaces between cells while glycerol cushions delicate cell membranes from rupturing, even as the caterpillar's heart and breathing pause completely beneath snow and subzero frost. When warm spring sunlight returns to the mossy forest floor, the caterpillar thaws, drinks morning dew, feeds on tender greens, and spins a soft silken cocoon.
