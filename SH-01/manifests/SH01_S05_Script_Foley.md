## Sanitized Continuous Narration Script

Centuries before mechanical escapement clocks chimed from European stone towers, people measured quiet night hours by the steady melting of pure beeswax. Historical timekeeping collections at the Smithsonian Institution describe ninth-century candle clocks associated with King Alfred the Great, who used six uniform twelve-inch beeswax tapers, each marked in one-inch divisions that burned cleanly in twenty minutes. To protect the flame from stone-hall drafts that would speed the wax, craftsmen enclosed each candle inside thin, translucent panels of scraped ox horn, creating an early windproof lantern. Builders even pressed small brass weight-pins into the wax at chosen hour marks so that as the warm beeswax softened, a pin slipped free and chimed softly into a bronze dish below.
