## Sanitized Continuous Narration Script

In nineteen hundred, sponge divers sheltering near the Greek island of Antikythera raised a corroded lump of sea-encrusted bronze from a two-thousand-year-old Roman shipwreck. Decades later, three-dimensional X-ray tomography documented by Smithsonian Magazine and the Science Museum Group revealed at least thirty interlocking bronze gearwheels hand-cut with millimeter-scale triangular teeth. Built by Hellenistic craftsmen in the second century B.C., the Antikythera Mechanism functioned as a shoebox-sized mechanical cosmos. Turning a single side crank drove epicyclic gear trains that modeled the Moon's variable orbital speed, tracked the nineteen-year Metonic calendar, and predicted solar and lunar eclipses decades in advance, preserving an extraordinary monument of ancient mathematical patience beneath the quiet Aegean seabed.
