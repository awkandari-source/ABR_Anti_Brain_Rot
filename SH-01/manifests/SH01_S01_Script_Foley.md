## Sanitized Continuous Narration Script

More than a mile beneath the sunlit surface of the East China Sea, in cold twilight darkness, lives an animal older than human agriculture. Meet Monorhaphis chuni, a deep-sea glass sponge anchored to the ocean floor by a single flexible rod of pure biological silica as thick as a pencil and nearly ten feet long. Marine researchers documented by the Smithsonian Ocean Portal and NOAA Ocean Exploration analyzed microscopic growth rings and oxygen isotopes locked inside one giant spicule, revealing an unbroken lifespan of roughly eleven thousand years. Layer by layer, this quiet filter feeder absorbed dissolved silicon from icy bottom currents that began flowing at the end of the last Ice Age, recording eleven millennia of ocean chemistry inside a living fiber-optic spine.
